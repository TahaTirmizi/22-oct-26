## Goal

Find one strong, recent, peer-reviewed research paper on an area of software engineering that is in high demand and hard for AI to replace. Read it, summarize it, and explain why that area holds up against automation.

## What counts as a good paper

- Peer-reviewed (journal or a major venue such as ICSE, FSE, ASE, CHI, IEEE S&P, USENIX) or a well-cited arXiv preprint
- Published 2023 or later, so it accounts for current AI coding tools
- About a skill area where human judgment, accountability, or context still matters

## Candidate areas to search

These are starting points, not final answers. Pick whichever has the best evidence.

| Area | Why it's hard to automate |
| ---- | ------------------------- |
| Software architecture and system design | Trade-offs depend on business context, scale, and organizational constraints |
| Security engineering and threat modeling | Adversarial, high-stakes, and someone has to be accountable |
| Requirements engineering | Turning unclear human needs into specifications takes negotiation |
| Safety-critical / regulated systems (medical, automotive, aviation) | Certification and legal responsibility stay with humans |
| AI engineering itself: evaluating, verifying, and governing AI systems | Demand grows as AI adoption grows |
| Distributed systems and reliability (SRE) | Incident response under uncertainty, in real production systems |
| Human-AI collaboration in software teams | Research on what developers still do better than AI tools |
